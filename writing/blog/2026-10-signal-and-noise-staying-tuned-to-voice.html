Signal and Noise: Staying Tuned to the Voice That Matters

October 20, 2026. Topics: Concerns, Encouragement, Information

What Is Signal and Noise?

The terms come from engineering and communications. The signal is the information you actually want to receive. The noise is everything else that interferes with it: static on a radio, background chatter in a crowded room, a weak cell connection that garbles a phone call.

What matters is the signal-to-noise ratio. If the signal is strong and the noise is low, the message comes through clearly. If the noise rises, or the signal weakens, the message gets distorted or lost. Nothing is wrong with the broadcast itself. The problem is on the receiving end.

We live in the noisiest period in human history. The average person is flooded daily with notifications, headlines, opinions, advertisements, and endless content. Most of it isn’t evil. Much of it is simply loud, urgent, and competing for the same attention God wants us to give to Him.

Signal and Noise in the Spiritual Life

In the spiritual life, the signal is the voice of God. It reaches us through His Word, through the Holy Spirit’s leading and conviction, and through prayer and fellowship with Him. The noise is anything that drowns that voice out or makes it hard to recognize.

Notice that noise isn’t always sinful. Some of it is:

  • Obvious distractions: entertainment, social media, endless scrolling, constant news.
  • Good things that become too loud: busyness, even busyness in ministry, church activity, and serving.
  • Other voices: opinions, trends, and teachings that sound spiritual but aren’t anchored in Scripture.
  • Internal noise: anxiety, unresolved guilt, pride, and the constant churn of our own thoughts.

Jesus described this in the parable of the sower. Some seed fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked the word. He named them: the cares of the world, the deceitfulness of riches, and the desires for other things (Mark 4:18–19). The seed was good. The soil was crowded. That is a signal-to-noise problem.

Scripture shows the same pattern repeatedly:

  • Elijah expected God in the wind, the earthquake, and the fire, but God spoke in a still small voice (1 Kings 19:11–13). The quiet signal was there the whole time, and Elijah had to get quiet enough to hear it.
  • Martha was “anxious and troubled about many things,” while Mary chose the one thing needed, sitting at Jesus’ feet (Luke 10:38–42). Martha wasn’t doing anything wrong. She was serving. But the noise of her service had crowded out the signal of His presence.
  • Jesus Himself modeled this. Early in the morning, while it was still dark, He went to a solitary place to pray (Mark 1:35). If the Son of God needed to deliberately step away from the noise, so do we.

The Heart of It: John 15

The strongest reason to care about this is found in John 15, where Jesus says, “I am the vine, ye are the branches… for without me ye can do nothing” (John 15:5, KJV).

The key word in the passage is abide, which Jesus uses repeatedly in the first ten verses. To abide means to remain, to stay connected, and to keep drawing life from the source. A branch doesn’t produce fruit by straining. It produces fruit by staying attached.

Here’s where signal and noise matters. Noise tempts us to believe that spiritual life is about output: doing more, serving more, producing more. Jesus says the opposite. Fruit is the result of connection, not a substitute for it. A branch that is busy but disconnected is just a dying branch.

Think of the vine as the signal and the branch as the receiver. Cut off from the source, no amount of activity produces life. This is why Jesus says “apart from me you can do nothing.” He doesn’t mean you can’t be busy. He means you can’t bear anything of eternal value without Him.

So maintaining a healthy relationship with Christ largely comes down to protecting the connection: reducing the noise that interferes with abiding, and strengthening the signal by feeding on His Word and staying in communion with Him.

Practical Ways to Increase the Signal and Reduce the Noise

Here are concrete steps you can take starting this week.

1. Audit your inputs

For one week, track what you take in each day: social media, news, podcasts, television, and conversations. Ask honestly: Does this draw me toward Christ, or does it dull my sensitivity to Him? You’ll probably find a few sources that account for most of the noise. Remove or limit them.

2. Give God the first part of your day

Before you check your phone, give your first attention to God. Even fifteen to twenty minutes with Scripture and prayer sets the tuning for the whole day. Whatever you open first tends to shape what you hear next.

3. Create a protected place and time

Choose a consistent time and place for being with God. Put your phone in another room or on airplane mode. If the phone is beside you, the noise is already in the room.

4. Read Scripture slowly

Noise trains us to skim. Counter that by reading less but more deeply. Take a short passage, such as a few verses of John 15, and read it several times. Ask: What does this say? What does it reveal about God? What is He asking of me? Memorize key verses so the signal goes with you through the day.

5. Make prayer a conversation

Many of us treat prayer as a monologue. Build in time for silence. After you’ve brought your requests, stop and listen. Write down what comes to mind: Scripture, convictions, or things you need to do or confess. Test everything against God’s Word.

6. Test the voices

Not every voice that sounds spiritual is from God. John writes, “Test the spirits to see whether they are from God” (1 John 4:1). Jesus says His sheep know His voice (John 10:27), and that familiarity comes from time spent listening. The better you know Scripture, the faster you’ll detect counterfeit signals.

7. Practice regular fasts from noise

Set aside a block of time each week, perhaps a Sabbath evening or a morning, to unplug from screens and media. Some people do an occasional longer retreat of a day or more. The goal isn’t legalism. It’s making room to hear.

8. Guard your calendar

Overcommitment is one of the biggest sources of spiritual noise. Learn to say no, even to good things, so you can say yes to the best thing. Ask whether your schedule has room for abiding.

9. End the day with reflection

Before bed, look back over the day. Where did I sense God’s presence? Where did I drift or get distracted? Where do I need to repent or give thanks? This habit sharpens your awareness of the signal over time.

10. Stay in community

Isolation makes us vulnerable to our own noise. Surround yourself with believers who will encourage you in the Word, pray with you, and speak honestly into your life.

A Warning and an Encouragement

The warning: you will not drift into a quiet life with God. Noise is the default, and the signal requires intentional protection. If you do nothing, the noise will win.

The encouragement: Jesus is not hard to find. He said, “Abide in me, and I in you.” He is the one who initiates and sustains the connection. Our part is to stay attached and remove what interferes. As we do, the fruit follows, not because we strained for it but because the life of the Vine is flowing through us.

Closing Reflection

This week, ask yourself:

  • What is the loudest noise in my life right now?
  • What is one thing I can remove, reduce, or silence to make room for God’s voice?
  • Am I abiding in Christ, or just busy for Him?

Start small, but start. In a world full of noise, let the voice of the Good Shepherd be the one you know best.
